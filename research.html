Research

Ultrafast and Magnetic-Resonance Spectroscopy

I have broad interests in ultrafast optical and magnetic resonance spectroscopies, including transient absorption, time-resolved Faraday rotation and ellipticity, pulsed EPR, and NMR. I am interested in applying these methods to reaction and spin dynamics in molecular systems, and I am currently working on tungsten(V) and molybdenum(V) complexes.

I have built several software tools to automate bottlenecks in this work, such as chirp correction and fitting complex CW-EPR spectra. They are all available on the Tools page.

I hope to keep working with these methods in graduate school and eventually in my own research group.

Electronic Structure of Excited States and Exotic Molecules

I am primarily interested in how electronic structure methods can be used to capture accurate descriptions of molecular excited states, particularly those that are too short-lived for thorough experimental characterization outside the (ultra)fast regimes. I am curious both about the application of existing electronic structure methods and the development of new methods. Most recently, I have been thinking about the development of quantitative (or at least qualitative) descriptions of Renner–Teller coupling in the 2Π state of the ethynyl radical (C2H•).

Photochemical and Electron-Transfer Dynamics

My first-ever formal research, in middle school and into high school, involved measuring rate constants associated with elementary photochemistry at the ZnO–water interface. I am now more interested in ultrafast phenomena in photochemical processes, namely photoinduced electron transfer. I am especially excited (no pun intended) about reactivity from higher-excited states in molecules (the controversial “anti-Kasha” behavior) and in understanding how spin dynamics and magnetic fields can influence these processes.
